Compose modern buildings through proportion, clear planes and useful interior space.
- Choose a few volumes with deliberate alignment or offset. Plan the rooms, entrance and circulation before adding cantilevers or terraces.
- Assign materials roles: concrete or quartz for planes, glass for openings, and wood or a darker block for selected surfaces. A limited palette helps edges read; the request may call for a warmer or more varied one.
- Flat and low roofs suit many examples. "rim" false and "eaves" false remove traditional roof trim. Preserve roof access, ceiling clearance and sensible connections to overhangs.
- Use window bands or larger glazed walls where rooms need them. Align mullions and solid piers with floor lines. Balance glass against solid walls instead of glazing every face equally.
- A cantilever should relate to the volume below; recess its support or show columns when needed. Keep projecting slabs and balconies out of entrances and stair headroom.
- Integrate lighting into ceilings, soffits and steps. A pool or roof terrace needs a purpose and enough space; neither is required for a modern house.
- Keep ornamental detail restrained unless a hybrid style is requested. Modern timber construction is valid; avoid automatically importing medieval framing and shutters.
