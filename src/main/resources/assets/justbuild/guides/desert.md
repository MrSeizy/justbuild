Choose a specific desert or Mediterranean building type and adapt it to the request.
- Desert compounds can use sandstone, mud brick or terracotta with shaded passages, courtyards and flat terraces. Mediterranean houses may use pale plaster-like walls and low red roofs. Avoid combining every regional motif by default.
- Arrange volumes around useful shade and circulation. Set back upper rooms or connect terraces with stairs; keep parapet openings and landings clear.
- Use wall thickness and recessed doors to give simple masses depth. Window size and placement can reinforce privacy, shade or a view.
- Wooden lattices, slab canopies and arcades can mark sheltered edges. Their supports belong beside the walking route.
- A dome on a drum, minaret or monumental entrance suits some public or religious buildings, not every desert house. Include the full dome and finial in the height allowance.
- For a villa, a low gable (pitch 0), dark window frames and a small loggia can establish character. Use a few related roof materials rather than random color changes.
- Courtyard planting, a basin or a pool should leave the main open space usable. Scale these to the site and blend the outer walls into the measured ground.
