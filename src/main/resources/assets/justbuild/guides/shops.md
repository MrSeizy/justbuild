Make a shop, tavern or workshop's activity legible from the street and usable inside.
- Plan a clear entrance, customer route and working area. Size the building for its trade and available site rather than forcing every shop or tavern into a fixed footprint.
- A stall can use posts, a counter and a small canopy. A larger shop may have an awning and display window; a tavern can have a hall and separate service space.
- An awning's stripes can use repeated wool lines. Keep its posts outside the approach and its underside above head height. Match its colors to the building.
- Counters of top slabs or upside-down stairs can divide public and working sides. Leave access behind them and to each chest, furnace or workstation.
- Signs and banners can mark the trade; block-only plans cannot supply sign text or other block-entity data. Suggest the activity with goods and equipment instead.
- A tavern may group tables, benches, a bar and a hearth around circulation. A smithy can use furnaces, an anvil, grindstone, smithing table and a water cauldron, with a supported hood or chimney.
- Stack goods in small, purposeful groups. Preserve doorways, stair landings and loading space; never use forbidden fire or lava for a forge.
