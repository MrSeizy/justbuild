Develop roof details after the main roof shape, clearance and junctions work.
- Give adjoining volumes related roofs at deliberate heights. A small building may need one simple roof; dormers and cross gables are optional.
- Choose pitch for the style and height allowance. "flare" creates a slab course at the eaves; "edge" accents the rim, ridge and corners; "beam" adds eave logs. Use a few of these with consistent materials.
- "mix" can vary related stairs blocks, such as deepslate tiles and cobbled deepslate. Keep variation restrained. A stripe of directional stairs needs explicit facing; a plain replace can lose orientation.
- Dormers need room below the main ridge. For a small example, place a one-wide window in the wall plane with posts at roof y through y+1; add a five-wide cross-axis gable starting at y+1, four deep inward, with rim/eaves false and trim null. Carve only the window column at the wall and one cell behind, then insert glass. Check the result against the actual slope.
- End a cross gable at the main ridge when that is the intended junction; going beyond it can project through the far slope. Keep its ridge lower unless a competing roof mass is intentional.
- Finials belong at selected peaks, with their full height inside bounds. "rim" false and "eaves" false make cleaner edges; plain roofs can be the right finish.
