Choose a species-like silhouette and fit the tree to its location.
- Establish trunk height, branching pattern and canopy outline together. A pine, willow and broad deciduous tree have different proportions; avoid one fixed canopy-to-height formula.
- All-bark wood blocks can keep branches visually continuous. Taper the trunk and limbs; small trees may need only one-block branches, while larger ones can flare at the base.
- Anchor roots to measured ground and spread a few irregularly into it. In a grove, give each trunk its own ground height and vary spacing and size.
- Split the trunk into a few limbs that support canopy clusters. Overlapping ellipsoids with gaps can make broad foliage; stacked narrowing clusters suit a conifer. Leave the branching direction visible in the overall shape.
- Use persistent=true for leaf blocks that should remain in a decorative custom canopy. Mix related leaves sparingly; wool or glass can stylize foliage when the request calls for it.
- Hanging leaves, vines or moss can reinforce a species or age. Keep entrances, paths and views clear beneath the canopy.
- A lantern or seat can make a garden tree a destination, but these are optional additions. Check the full crown against neighbouring roofs and the selected boundary.
