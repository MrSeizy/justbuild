Design a castle as a connected defensive enclosure with inhabited buildings inside.
- Plan the gate, curtain walls, projecting towers, keep and open courtyard together. Reserve routes and stairs before filling the enclosure with buildings. Let the terrain and requested plan determine symmetry.
- Massing: broad masonry bases, thick wall faces and towers rising above the curtains establish strength. A keep can dominate through width and position as well as height; never multiply tower heights to size it. Lower halls and service wings make a useful hierarchy.
- Towers project beyond the wall line so their sides are visible. Make their interiors large enough for stairs, landings and an upper room; wall walks enter through clear openings.
- A substantial curtain can carry a two- or three-wide clear walk behind a parapet. Use a readable merlon-and-gap rhythm, corbels where the top projects, and stairs from the courtyard. Reduce the extent if these cannot fit.
- Make the gatehouse a deliberate interruption in the curtain: a passage beneath a room, or flanking towers. A raised portcullis leaves the walking opening clear.
- Choose battlemented stone tops, modest pitched roofs or taller fantasy spires to suit the request. Reserve timber framing for appropriate halls or upper rooms; a fortified keep can remain masonry.
- Use restrained stone variation, deep entrances and narrow lower openings. Add larger windows to protected residential faces. A well, service yard and a few planted areas give the courtyard purpose without consuming its routes.
