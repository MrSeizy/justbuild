Establish a vessel's hull, decks and rig before adding maritime detail.
- Choose the type and intended scale: a broad cargo ship, slender longship and small boat need different proportions. Fit the hull and any masts within the full height and width budget.
- A bowl-shaped ellipsoid can start a curved hull; refine the bow, stern and waterline with narrower sections, stairs or slabs. Place decks deliberately rather than filling the entire upper volume solid.
- Plan stairs and hatches between inhabited decks. Leave walking width along the rail, around masts and through cabins. A raised stern deck needs an actual route to it.
- Use related woods for hull and deck, with a limited contrasting band or trim. A flat stern and windowed cabin may suit a galleon; a longship can taper at both ends.
- Choose mast number from the ship's type and size. Vary mast heights, then fit yards, sails and rigging so the hull remains legible. Thin wool sails can bow forward and narrow upward.
- Rails, ports, a bowsprit and a few deck fittings explain function. Keep ropes and equipment out of routes and headroom.
- Match the hull's position to the measured water surface or requested display base. Decorative ships do not gain simulated movement from their shape.
