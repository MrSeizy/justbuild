Build Gothic character through vertical bays, pointed openings and a structured silhouette.
- For a church, lay out nave, aisles, crossing and entrance before towers. Other Gothic buildings can use the same bay rhythm without adopting a cathedral plan.
- Let piers and buttresses align with interior structure. Project buttresses where space allows and step them inward as they rise; leave doors and paths clear between them.
- Use pointed arches for selected doors or windows. Narrow paired lancets, a larger entrance window or a rose window can make focal points; size them to the facade rather than imposing fixed dimensions.
- Stone, tuff, deepslate and restrained contrasting trim support readable depth. Stained glass can reinforce a small color family or the requested mood.
- Steep roofs and spires can accent major volumes. Keep tower heights within the overall scale and budget; avoid multiplying the nave height into an impractical needle.
- Pinnacles and finials belong at structural or compositional accents. A few well-placed ones make a clearer skyline than spikes on every corner.
- Occupied towers need stairs and landings, and a large hall needs clear interior circulation. Use lighting to reveal the space while preserving its intended atmosphere.
