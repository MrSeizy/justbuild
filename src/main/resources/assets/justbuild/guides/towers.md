Choose a tower's form from its purpose: defence, lookout, bell, beacon, residence or ornament.
- Establish the usable interior first. Wall thickness, a staircase and its landings must leave room at the destination. A taller shaft needs real intermediate floors and connections when occupied.
- Square towers use wall planes or hollow fills; round ones can use hollow cylinders with separate floors. Anchor the base and give projecting upper stages visible support.
- Vary height relative to neighbouring buildings. A stout wall tower and a slender spire need different proportions; no fixed height-to-width ratio fits both.
- Choose the top deliberately: a parapet for a fighting platform, a lantern room for a lighthouse, roof skirts for a pagoda, or a pointed roof for a spire.
- For a spire, roof style "spire" controls the taper; set height for the available skyline budget. Its default is tall. Include overhangs and any finial in the full bounds. A round shaft may need a square roof platform.
- Place openings by use: narrow lower slits on defensive towers, larger openings in a lookout or bell chamber. Let floor lines and a few corbels articulate the shaft rather than covering it with trim.
- Connect wall walks through the tower with clear landings; a decorative doorway alone does not make that route usable.
