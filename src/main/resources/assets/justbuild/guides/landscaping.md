Shape useful open space around the requested buildings or natural features.
- Establish paths between actual destinations: doors, gates, work areas and viewpoints. Join each threshold at its walking height. Choose width, bends and approach length from the available space; a tiny yard does not need a five-block straight run.
- Follow measured terrain with local segments and supported steps. Broader end supports help bridges meet both banks; reserve deck clearance and place rails beside the route. Keep arches and underslung lights clear of the crossing.
- Use related path materials, such as dirt_path with coarse_dirt and podzol, or stone_bricks with stone and mossy stone. Low verges can soften edges without blocking travel.
- Group planting in beds or clusters and retain deliberate open space. Choose species, density and tree size for the setting rather than putting the same garden around every build.
- Water features need a contained bed and banks. A small courtyard may suit a basin or well; a larger park can carry a pond or fountain. Make the water follow the intended shape instead of spreading into routes.
- Rocks can be overlapping ellipsoids partly buried in the ground, with vegetation on gentler ledges. Blend terrain changes into existing slopes.
- Place benches and lights where people stop or turn. Fit them around views, stairs and entrances; spacing is a design choice, not a fixed grid.
