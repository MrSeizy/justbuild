Design urban buildings as occupied floors within a coherent street frontage.
- Choose the footprint, entrance and vertical circulation before repeating floors. A small apartment block, office tower and hotel need different proportions and room layouts.
- Build a usable floor module with sufficient headroom, stair openings and a landing. "repeat" can copy it upward; connect the last flight to the final floor and roof where access is requested.
- Organize the facade into a base, repeated middle and a deliberate top. A taller lobby, podium or setback can distinguish these without adding unrelated volumes.
- Align piers, window bands and floor lines. Concrete, stone or terracotta can form the frame, with glass between; glazing density should suit the rooms and requested style.
- Place balconies against occupied rooms with an opening and a safe edge. A decorative slab outside a sealed wall is not a usable balcony.
- A flat roof may carry rails, plant rooms or an antenna. Keep equipment grouped and leave roof circulation clear.
- Relate the building to pavements, neighbouring entrances and any courtyard. Give ground-level activity and the main doorway enough emphasis to read from the street.
