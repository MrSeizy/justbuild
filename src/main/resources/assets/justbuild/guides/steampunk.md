Organize steampunk machinery around a building's structure and an understandable purpose.
- Start with the main volumes and working routes. A workshop, station and airship need different layouts; pipes and gears should not substitute for the building itself.
- Brick, dark timber, stone and selected copper make a useful palette. Assign each a role and use related oxidation states where weathering is intended. Waxed copper preserves a chosen state.
- Group machinery into readable systems: a chimney and boiler, a lifting arm, a ventilation stack or a pipe run connecting equipment. Support projected components instead of scattering them across every face.
- Iron_chain, lightning rods, grates, hoppers and cauldrons can suggest cables, ducts or machinery. Keep moving or working mechanisms descriptive unless actual mechanics are requested and supported.
- Fit tall stacks, roof equipment and balconies within the skyline and access plan. Doors, stairs and workstations need space after pipes and rails are added.
- An airship needs a coherent hull, lifting envelope and suspension connections. Size these together rather than applying a fixed balloon-to-hull ratio.
- Use a few copper bulbs or lanterns at work areas and entrances. Leave quiet surfaces around the major mechanical features.
