Plan agricultural buildings around storage, work and movement.
- A barn needs a clear main aisle and large usable doors; stables need individual bays opening onto that aisle. Reserve these before hay stacks, stalls or loft supports.
- Set post spacing from the structural bays. Spruce, oak, stripped logs and a stone base suit rustic examples. A red barn can use red terracotta with pale corner and door trim; neither palette is mandatory.
- Choose a gable or gambrel roof for the required volume. A hayloft needs an accessible stair and landing, adequate headroom and protected openings. A high decorative hay door alone is not access.
- Doors, loft openings and pulley beams can make the working front recognizable. Keep the loading area outside unobstructed.
- Silos are separate storage volumes with a cylinder and cap; include them only when requested or useful. A windmill needs a distinct tower, hub and clear sail silhouette, with space for the blades.
- Group troughs, hay, barrels, grindstones and tools by activity. Place fences and gates to connect yards and entrances rather than dividing routes accidentally.
- Fit the number of outbuildings to the site. A coherent working yard is more useful than a collection of unrelated farm decorations.
