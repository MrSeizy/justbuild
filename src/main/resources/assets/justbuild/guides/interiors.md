Furnish rooms around their purpose and a connected route from the entrance.
- Lay out circulation first, including doors, stairs, landings and access to storage. Put furniture along its edges. Avoid isolated floor pockets behind beds, chests and shelves; check the remaining walking space.
- Give each room or open-plan zone one main activity. A hearth, desk or window seat can be its focus; rugs, screens or changes in floor material can separate activities without walls.
- Seating: stair chairs or benches, optionally with trapdoor arms. Tables: top slabs, or a fence with a closed trapdoor or pressure plate above. Choose dimensions that leave room to stand beside them.
- Storage: chests, barrels, bookshelves, shelves and pots against walls. Keep a reachable standing space at each chest or workstation and beside beds; leave lids and door swings clear where applicable.
- A kitchen can group a smoker, furnace, counter and cauldron sink. A bedroom needs a bed and reachable storage. A library or study can use shelves, a lectern and a reading table. Use only the activities requested.
- Floors, ceiling beams and a few lights should reinforce the room's scale. Check vertical clearance after hanging lights and adding rugs or floor changes.
- For an interior-only edit, retain the existing walls, floors, roof and usable doors unless the request explicitly changes them. Add furniture without turning the room into a new building.
