Integrate a chimney with its hearth, wall and roof.
- Choose brick, stone or dark masonry that relates to the building. The shaft can stand against an exterior wall or pass through the roof; give it a continuous, supported route.
- Size the shaft for the building. A narrow one- or two-block example suits a small house; a large hall or workshop can need a broader stack. Do not apply one proportion everywhere.
- A stepped base or a few corbels can connect the stack to the wall. Let it rise enough above the nearby roof to read clearly, within the height allowance.
- Wall blocks can suggest chimney pots. An optional campfire provides smoke; a hay bale below increases its height. Treat this as a visual effect, not a working ventilation simulation.
- A fireplace needs an opening, a hearth and a mantel that leave circulation clear. Use a campfire or a light block where appropriate; never use forbidden fire or lava blocks.
- Add the roof penetration deliberately and check that later roof or trim operations do not seal the hearth, erase the stack or leave gaps around it.
