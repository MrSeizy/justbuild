Use ordered bays and a clear base, column zone, entablature and roof for a classical composition.
- Set the main axis and entrance first. A temple can have surrounding columns; a civic hall may need only a portico. Choose the arrangement for the request rather than copying one monument.
- Fit column spacing to the available width and doorway. Repeated shafts need a visible base and capital, with enough clearance to walk between them. One-block columns suit small builds; larger shafts need wider bays.
- Quartz, sandstone or calcite can make a coherent main material, with restrained stone or terracotta accents. Fluting can be suggested by pillars instead of many small trim blocks.
- A low gable (pitch 0) and filled pediment suit a temple front. Keep the horizontal band above the columns in proportion; a small building needs only a few layers.
- A raised platform needs broad, connected stairs and clear landings. Preserve a useful interior behind the portico, with light and an obvious doorway.
- Roman-inspired work may use arches, arcades or domes. An amphitheatre needs connected entrances, circulation rings and stepped seating, not just repeated outer arches.
- Place statues or braziers only when they fit the brief and do not crowd the approach.
