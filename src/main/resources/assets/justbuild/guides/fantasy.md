Choose one imaginative premise, then make its shape and construction readable.
- Establish a main silhouette: a crooked tower, mushroom house, suspended hall or overgrown ruin. Give supporting forms a clear relationship to it; every roof does not need to compete for attention.
- Use asymmetry or exaggeration intentionally. A leaning upper volume still needs the support, magical suspension or connection implied by the request.
- A flared spire, unusual roof material or long finial can carry the idea. Include overhangs and tips in the bounds. Select a few material families and repeat their roles.
- For a mushroom building, size the cap around the usable stem interior and entrance. Overlapping dome or ellipsoid forms can give a varied cap; spots and hanging lights remain secondary.
- Elven, fairy and wizard buildings need not share one palette. Let the request choose light stone, wood, dark masonry or saturated accents, with openings and balconies that serve rooms.
- Inhabited fantasy buildings still need usable floors, stairs, landings and headroom. Shape the unusual exterior around those routes instead of forcing routes into a finished sculpture.
- Overgrowth can frame selected edges or explain age. Preserve the main silhouette, entrance and intended open space.
