Use a consistent Japanese or Chinese-inspired building type rather than mixing motifs indiscriminately.
- Establish the platform, timber bays and room layout before the roof. Choose dark timber, pale infill, red framing or another palette appropriate to the request.
- Broad roof surfaces and repeated eave lines can carry the silhouette. A low roof (pitch 0) is one option; choose the pitch, overhang and corner treatment for the specific type and available space.
- Upturned corners can be suggested with a small raised stair or slab at the eave end. Keep the rest of the edge coherent and avoid attaching a large ornament to every corner.
- Verandas need enough walking width after posts and rails. Connect their stairs and entrances through clear openings in the platform edge.
- A pagoda uses a deliberate sequence of tiers and roof skirts. Fit the stairs, floors and headroom before repeating tiers; reduce the tier count when the height budget is small.
- A Japanese-inspired castle can use a broad stone base and stepped upper volumes with repeated roof lines. A torii is a separate gate composition with two posts and crossbeams; neither belongs in every house design.
- Lanterns, screens, courtyard planting and water can reinforce the setting when requested. Keep their scale subordinate to the architecture.
