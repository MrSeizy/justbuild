Use small blocks to explain a build's structure, purpose or age.
- Choose a few recurring details rather than applying every technique. Leave quiet surfaces so important entrances, forms and materials remain legible.
- Trapdoors and signs can suggest shutters, panels or furniture sides; fences and gates can make brackets or rails. Check their orientation and clearance in the finished build.
- Barrels, pots, shelves and workstations suggest activities. Group them where that activity happens and leave access to each usable item.
- Lanterns can hang from beams or short iron_chain runs. Buttons can mark occasional bolts; banners can identify a gate or hall.
- Weathering should follow plausible locations: moss near damp bases, vines at sheltered edges, wear along a path. Use related materials and sparse clusters rather than uniform visual noise.
- Scale details to viewing distance and available blocks. A tiny cottage may need only a door frame, one window treatment and a light. Large walls benefit first from openings and structural bays.
- Recheck paths, stairs and headroom after decoration. Details may refine a finished layout, never replace its necessary structure or connections.
